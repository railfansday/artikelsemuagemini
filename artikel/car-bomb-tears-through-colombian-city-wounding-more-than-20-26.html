The explosion ripped through one of the city's central squares.

A car bomb has exploded in the central Colombian city of Villavicencio, injuring at least 23 people.

The explosive device was detonated in front of the offices of the regional government, in the city centre, damaging several buildings.

No group has so far admitted carrying out the attack - the latest in a series using explosives to target official buildings and security forces' headquarters.

Colombian President Abelardo de la Espriella blamed "narcoterrorism", but gave no details. Armed criminal groups have recently stepped up attacks in what appears to be retaliation for an offensive ordered against them by De la Espriella.

Writing on social media, the president stated that his government would "bluntly and forcefully" respond, external to "every drop of Colombian blood spilled".

"There will be no impunity and no retreat," he added.

It is the latest in a series of attacks targeting official buildings and security forces' headquarters.

Three weeks ago, three adults and a baby were killed in a car bomb attack near army barracks in Tolima province.

And earlier on Monday, drones dropped explosives on the police headquarters in the northern city of CÃºcuta.

The car bomb in Villavicencio went off on Monday evening - the eve of the 10th anniversary of the signing of a peace deal between the government and left-wing rebels of the Revolutionary Armed Forces of Colombia (Farc), Colombia's largest guerrilla group.

Following the signing of the deal, thousands of left-wing rebel fighters laid down their arms.

However, a significant number of Farc members, who did not agree with the deal, refused to disarm and joined dissident groups instead.

While forced displacement, child recruitment and the numbers of attacks on security forces dropped in the aftermath of the deal, there has been an upsurge in recent years driven by dissident Farc factions and criminal groups fighting for control of drug trafficking, illegal mining and extortion rackets.

Colombia's first left-wing president, who was in power from August 2022 to August 2026, tried to negotiate with these armed groups as part of what he called his "total peace" strategy.

However, the talks stalled and collapsed with several of the criminal groups violating ceasefires they had agreed to observe.

De la Espriella was elected on a promise to crack down on "narcoterrorism" and has been scathing about his predecessor's approach.

He has also been critical of the 2016 agreement, calling it a "false peace".

In a post on X, uploaded shortly before news of the car bomb in Villavicencio spread, he referred to the peace deal as a "mess", describing it as a "waste of public resources" and the "cause behind Colombia's ruin".

Former President Juan Manuel Santos and Rodrigo LondoÃ±o - the two men who signed the peace deal on behalf of the Colombian government and the Farc respectively - defended it in an interview with BBC Mundo ahead of the anniversary.

Santos said that De la Espriella was "totally wrong" to call it a "false peace".

"He wants to use, or has bought into that cynical and mistaken theory that the violence we see today is the result of the peace deal, when the exact opposite is the case," Santos mentioned, arguing that the problem lies with the lack of implementation of the agreement.
