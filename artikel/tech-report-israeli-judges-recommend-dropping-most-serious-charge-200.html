Judges in Israel have suggested prosecutors drop bribery charges against Benjamin Netanyahu, potentially handing the caretaker prime minister a boost as he fights for his political survival.

The judges told the attorney general that there was little chance of conviction in the first of three cases he is facing, in which he is accused of granting government favours in exchange for positive media coverage.

They also questioned whether his actions amounted to criminal conduct in a second corruption case, and suggested prosecutors seek a plea deal in a third.

Netanyahu has consistently denied the allegations against him and called on Wednesday for the trial to be ended "immediately".

"Ten years of terrible suffering for me, my family, my son," he noted in a televised statement.

"Ten years of incitement to hatred that wounds the soul.".

Netanyahu was first charged in 2019, but court proceedings have been prolonged in part by the recent Israel-Gaza war.

The case now under serious doubt, known as Case 4,000, concerns an alleged "reciprocal arrangement" between Netanyahu and the controlling shareholder in Israel's biggest telecoms manufacturer. Both denied any wrongdoing.

In a highly unusual step, a judging panel at the Jerusalem District Court summoned Israeli Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara on Wednesday to express their concerns.

While they left open the possibility of a conviction in the third case: Case 1,000, which alleges Netanyahu acted for the benefit of an Australian billionaire after being gifted cigars and champagne by him.

However, the judges told the attorney general that, in the public interest, there needed to be a "rethink" in how the trial should move forward, Israeli media report.

Baharav-Miara said she would consider the judges' recommendations.

It has been six years since Netanyahu became Israel's first sitting prime minister to face a criminal trial.

He has always claimed to be the victim of a political witch-hunt and noted on Wednesday that the "fabricated cases" against him were now falling apart.

The cases have continued to progress in court while Netanyahu remained in power throughout the war in Gaza, though judges agreed to reduce the number of hearings due to security concerns around the conflict.

The accusations against him have rocked Israeli politics, fuelling already deep divisions. His trial has coincided with controversial, far-reaching judiciary reforms by Netanyahu's government.

For his opponents, Netanyahu's fear of prison has distorted his decision-making as prime minister and made him especially dependent on his far-right coalition partners to remain in power.

This latest development will give a boost to Netanyahu, whose political opponent Gadi Eisenkot is currently attempting to form a coalition government after an inconclusive general election last month.

According to recent reports, while Netanyahu's parliamentary bloc won more seats than that of his rival, both fell short of an outright majority. Israel's president has offered Eisenkot the first chance to form a new administration.

Netanyahu remains subject to an arrest warrant from the International Criminal Court for alleged war crimes and crimes against humanity in Gaza, which he also denies.
